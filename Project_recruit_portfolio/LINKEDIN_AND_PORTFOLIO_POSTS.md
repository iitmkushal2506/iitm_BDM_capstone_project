**Text to copy:**

Most data projects start with a downloaded CSV from Kaggle. 

For my BDM Capstone at IIT Madras, I decided to do something different: I stepped directly onto the factory floor of an established, 3-generation **medical equipment & surgical implant manufacturer**.

Over 3 on-site plant visits, I audited 17 months of physical registers, QC rejection logs, and cost sheets, digitizing 119 production batches across 7 precision orthopedic product lines.

The company was facing 3 classic manufacturing dilemmas:
1️⃣ Defect rates creeping up from 1.00% to 1.63% across 1,52,000+ units.
2️⃣ Rising but unquantified rework & scrap losses.
3️⃣ Disruptive stockouts in essential surgical items during peak hospital demand.

Here is the most fascinating counter-intuitive insight the data revealed:

💡 **Defect frequency does NOT equal financial risk.**

The factory floor team spent most of their time firefighting *Needle Holders* and *Intramedullary Nails* because they had the highest defect rates (~1.57%). 

However, running a **One-Way ANOVA (p < 0.001)** and Cost of Poor Quality (COPQ) modeling revealed that the *Plaster Cutting Saw*—which had a low defect rate of only 0.97%—drove **62.2% (₹7.48 Lakhs) of the total ₹12.02 Lakhs financial loss** simply because each unit costs ₹12,000 to manufacture.

Fixing the low-cost high-defect items was giving the illusion of progress, while the high-value items were quietly leaking working capital.

To solve this end-to-end, I built:
✅ **Quality Engineering**: SPC X-bar & R control limits + 4M Ishikawa RCA across 6 failure modes to address systemic machine calibration drift.
✅ **Financial Analytics**: Parametric hypothesis testing & COPQ isolation isolating ₹5.5L+ in annual recoverable losses.
✅ **Supply Chain Analytics**: Dynamic ABC-EOQ-ROP replenishment models coupled with **ARIMA demand forecasting**, guaranteeing a 95% service level to prevent future stockouts.

Data science isn't just about training algorithms—it's about understanding business mechanics, asking the right questions on the shop floor, and translating numbers into decisions that protect margins.

Grateful to the plant operations leadership for their mentorship and collaboration, and the faculty mentors at the IIT Madras BS Program.
